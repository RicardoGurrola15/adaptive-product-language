# Contextual adaptation

## Detect before writing

Build a compact context card from supplied evidence: **product**, **audience**, **domain**, **surface**, **objective**, **risk**, **locale**, and **flow stage**. Mark each as declared, verified, inferred, or unknown. Resolve conflicts by the skill's evidence precedence. Ask one blocking question or state the limitation when an unknown material fact would change a claim; do not fill it with generic language.

## Claim boundary before composition

Create a private claim ledger before drafting. Put every concrete product statement into one of three buckets:

- **Allowed:** explicitly declared or behaviorally verified in the supplied context.
- **Conditional:** plausible but not confirmed; omit it from visible copy unless the user authorizes a clearly qualified statement.
- **Forbidden:** contradicted, unknown, or stronger than the evidence; never write it.

Visible copy MUST use only the allowed bucket. Do not infer common capabilities from the domain or from a familiar interface pattern. In particular, never add a cart, checkout, filters, comparison tools, inventory, delivery, financing, certifications, warranty coverage, technical inspection steps, performance details, or measurable savings unless each one is explicitly evidenced. A section that is required but lacks evidence must use a truthful, general description or be flagged as blocked; it must not be completed with a typical feature.

Treat every heading, benefit, CTA, FAQ answer, reassurance, and button label as a claim-bearing surface. Preserve the exact strength of the evidence: a declared **policy** does not authorize coverage details, defect guarantees, duration, eligibility, or outcomes. Prefer "consulta las condiciones de la garantía" over inventing what the guarantee covers. Do not turn "accessible pricing" into "real savings" or any comparative claim without evidence.

Before returning copy, run a claim-diff pass: for each concrete noun or promise, ask "Where is this supported?" Remove unsupported additions rather than explaining them. This pass is mandatory even when the user requests marketing language and even when the inferred feature would be common for the product category.

## Adapt, do not template

| Domain / situation | Adapt the language to | Protect |
|---|---|---|
| Legal / lawyers | jurisdiction-aware task, matter status, decision consequence | no legal outcome or compliance claim without evidence |
| Health / clinicians | care task, patient-safe clarity, uncertainty and next step | no diagnosis, efficacy, or urgency claim without evidence |
| SaaS | role, workflow, setup stage, product action and system state | no feature, integration, uptime, or automation claim without evidence |
| E-commerce | item, variant, price/availability certainty, purchase stage | no stock, delivery, discount, or guarantee claim without evidence |
| Marketplace | side of market, listing detail, trust boundary, transaction stage | no verification, matching, or outcome promise without evidence |
| Finance | instrument, decision horizon, fees/risk disclosure, regulated action | no return, approval, security, or suitability promise without evidence |
| Creator tools | creative task, audience, publishing stage, ownership/control | no reach, earnings, virality, or rights claim without evidence |

Choose wording from the product's evidenced concepts and the audience's task. The same action may need a different register, detail level, caution, and CTA at discovery, comparison, decision, setup, recovery, or post-action stages. This matrix is a reasoning aid, not a set of templates or required phrases.

## Warning signals, not word bans

Treat clichés, generic superlatives, vague reassurance, artificial urgency, jargon, and robotic parallelism as **signals to inspect context**. Replace or retain them only after checking whether they obscure the task, evidence, risk, or locale. They are never a blacklist, and no preferred word earns quality by itself. Hard rules remain factual: preserve declared behavior and terms, use the requested locale, disclose material uncertainty, and do not invent claims.

## Generic / robotic review

For every coordinated surface, ask: does it name the actual user task, distinguish this product from a similar one, maintain the same proposition and truth boundary across sections, and sound like a person addressing this audience in this locale? Remove filler, unsupported certainty, repeated empty claims, and mechanical slogan patterns only when they fail those checks.

## Variation rule

Do not reuse a universal brand voice, CTA formula, or stock benefit pattern across products. Re-derive the message hierarchy, evidence detail, register, and action framing from the context card. Similar language is acceptable only when the product task and evidence genuinely match.
