# Terminology

## Glossary workflow

1. Extract repeated concepts and variants from the requested surface or audit.
2. Identify the canonical term from existing product usage or ask when none is authoritative.
3. Record source-language term, locale-specific rendering, meaning, and prohibited ambiguity.
4. Check labels, help, errors, navigation, SEO, and marketing surfaces together.
5. Change terminology only within the authorized scope; report remaining variants.

## Term decision table

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Is it a product name or protected label? | Preserve it unless the owner explicitly authorizes localization. |
| Does the same concept have multiple verbs? | Choose the verb matching the actual outcome and use it across states. |
| Does the translation change scope or permission? | Stop and ask; this is a semantic/product decision. |
| Is a short term ambiguous in a control? | Add the outcome or contextual noun rather than relying on a generic verb. |
| Is the difference locale-specific? | Keep distinct locale entries; do not force one spelling into every locale. |

## Practical rules

- Prefer one canonical term per concept per locale.
- Keep singular/plural and gender agreement consistent where the language requires it.
- Preserve placeholders and variable names exactly.
- Do not create a glossary merely to justify rewriting accurate copy.
- Explain intentional exceptions, such as a legal or branded term, in the finding evidence or problem.
