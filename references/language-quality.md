# Language quality

Run all eight checks for each proposed or audited surface.

| Check | Question | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic | Does the text mean what the product actually does? | “Save” on an action that only previews. |
| Coherence | Does it agree with nearby copy and other states? | “Remove” in one place and “Delete permanently” in another for the same action. |
| Congruence | Does wording match control, visual emphasis, timing, and consequence? | A destructive action styled and labeled like a harmless secondary action. |
| Linguistic | Is it grammatical, readable, context-specific, and natural for the language? | Literal translation, vague noun-heavy sentence, or generic copy that could describe any product. |
| Localization | Does it fit locale conventions, units, dates, currency, pluralization, and register? | English punctuation or US-only terminology in es-419. |
| Accessibility | Can users understand, locate, operate, and recover from it? | “Something went wrong” with no recovery action; icon-only meaning. |
| Marketing-transparency | Are price, value, eligibility, timing, limits, and uncertainty clear? | A promotion foregrounds savings but hides cadence or eligibility. |
| Anti-dark-pattern | Does the copy preserve informed choice and an easy decline? | “No, I hate saving money” as a guilt-loaded opt-out. |

## Review order

1. Truth and user consequence.
2. Recovery and accessibility.
3. Coherence across the flow.
4. Locale and linguistic quality.
5. Transparency and persuasion.
6. Preference-level polish.

A fluent sentence that fails truth, recovery, or informed choice is not acceptable. Do not score a specific word, cliché, or style marker positively or negatively in isolation; assess its fit to the product context.
