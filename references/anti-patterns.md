# Anti-patterns

These are contextual warning signals, not a blacklist or mandatory-vocabulary inverse. Inspect the product task, audience, risk, locale, and evidence before changing any phrase.

Reject these approaches even when they sound polished:

- **Generic fluency:** rewriting without checking the action, state, audience, or product truth.
- **Silent locale choice:** returning copy without declaring inferred or defaulted locale.
- **Literal translation:** preserving word order while losing intent, register, or consequence.
- **Mixed active UI:** leaving Spanish and English mixed because one key was convenient.
- **False certainty:** inventing a recovery action, price, entitlement, date, or guarantee.
- **Technical leakage:** exposing stack traces, internal IDs, or implementation terms to users.
- **Vague error:** saying “Something went wrong” without diagnosis or available recovery.
- **CTA mismatch:** labeling a preview, save, submit, or destructive action inaccurately.
- **Price fog:** omitting currency, unit, cadence, renewal, eligibility, limits, or material conditions.
- **Urgency theater:** countdowns, “last chance,” or scarcity not supported by truth.
- **Guilt opt-out:** shame, insult, or identity pressure on a decline action.
- **Obstructed refusal:** hiding, disabling, or visually demoting a reasonable decline path.
- **Over-localization:** changing product names, identifiers, or intentional terms without evidence.
- **Audit sprawl:** reporting every preference as a defect and burying urgent findings.
- **Unauthorized remediation:** changing behavior, legal meaning, product policy, or unrelated files during a copy task.
